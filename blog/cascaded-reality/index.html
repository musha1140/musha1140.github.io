Cascaded reality, or: the universe as a commit log

In September 2024 I wrote a draft about quantum mechanics, black holes, and the multiverse. It got two things wrong, and I'm opening with them because the method matters more than the conclusions: I claimed information could outrun light (it can't, the no-communication theorem closes that door), and I said black holes form when massive stars collapse (that's one way, and as of last year it's provably not the only one). This is the corrected, tightened, blog-voice version. The full revision, with all forty-nine references and the claims ledger intact, lives beside this post, the thesis itself.

The cascade

Here's the core idea in one breath: the definiteness of everyday reality, the fact that your coffee cup has a position instead of a smear of them, is not painted onto the world at the bottom. It accumulates.

Quantum systems in contact with an environment get "measured" constantly: air molecules, photons, neighboring matter. Only certain states survive that monitoring (Zurek called the filtering einselection), and the environment doesn't just destroy the fragile states, it broadcasts redundant copies of the survivor. Many observers each intercept a different fragment and read the same record. That agreement is the entire content of the word "objective."

I call the resulting structure cascaded reality: outcomes fix, records propagate, and every record constrains everything downstream. Once the copies are everywhere, the outcome cannot be unwritten, not because the underlying law is irreversible (it isn't), but because you'd have to chase down every copy. The classical world is the quantum world's commit log.

(If you've pressed the valve on this site's homepage: that's the same mechanics, played, and played honestly. Each press runs a 16-qubit register through Hadamard gates in the quantum-circuit package and measures it; the collapse of that register decides which section of the page comes into existence, or whether the press hits a null field and nothing does. The null-field odds are not hand-picked either: they derive from the SHA-256 digest of the first line of the Equation, the same "ĤΨ=0" that seeds the thesis companion's hash chain. The constraint sets the statistics, the measurement collapses them, the record keeps the outcome. Sections don't exist until measured; once measured, the record keeps them.)

The singularity, inverted

General relativity defines a singularity by what it costs the theory: equations stop returning answers. That's the outside view, singularity as breakdown.

I read it inverted. At a horizon, entropy saturates the Bekenstein bound: the maximum information the region can hold, exactly a quarter of the area in Planck units. Saturation is not failure; it's a ledger closed. And time ends there for a specific reason: time is relational bookkeeping between subsystems (Page and Wootters showed how a timeless whole yields evolution for embedded clocks, it's been demonstrated on entangled photons). When correlations are complete, there are no further distinguishable states for a clock to count. The singularity is one position after one event: the constraint saturated, the cascade complete, the equation solved.

The deepest equation we have for the whole universe, Wheeler-DeWitt, has no time in it at all. ĤΨ = 0. Not a law of evolution; a self-consistency condition. The Big Bang, on this reading, isn't the input fed to the equation. It's what the solution looks like from a clock embedded inside it.

The loop, and what 2026 changed

Chain the pieces: a timeless constraint, a cascade that writes records, a saturation point where the ledger closes, and then Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology and Smolin's cosmological natural selection both propose, in different formalisms, that the closed ledger hands forward the next initial condition. I call the assembled loop the Feedback Equation, and I label it plainly: speculation.

But its substrate improved on August 12, 2026. Naidu and collaborators reported MoM-BH*-1 in Nature: an object 660 million years after the Big Bang, read as a ~100,000-solar-mass black hole growing inside a hydrogen envelope, a black hole star. Not a stellar corpse: a native, star-like citizen of the early universe, possibly the swaddled first phase of most supermassive black holes. A universe that grows black holes as a first-class process is structurally better built for feedback than the one in any textbook printed before 2023.

Meanwhile the boundary terms of the whole loop are under live renegotiation: the local and early-universe measurements of the expansion rate refuse to overlap at roughly five sigma, and DESI keeps preferring dark energy that evolves. Wrong decade to declare the equation's edges settled.

No shortcut

One more claim, the newest in the ledger, conditional irreducibility: wherever an outcome routes through a decoherence-grade record channel, no computation cheaper than execution exists. You cannot skip to the state at year 13.8 billion; the running is the solving. The lab-bench version is a cryptographic hash, preimage resistance is engineered irreducibility, and I measured the field version myself: in MDTGF, a convergence gate upstream of SHA-256 could not predict primality downstream of it, ever. The gate schedules execution. It cannot replace it.

The claim is deliberately conditional, physics is full of compressible regularities; that's what laws are, and it's falsifiable: any demonstrated shortcut across such a channel breaks it.

The ledger

Standing of everything above, in three lines, because a claim doesn't count until its label does:

  • Established: decoherence, einselection, quantum Darwinism; the Bekenstein-Hawking area law; Wheeler-DeWitt; Page-Wootters relational time; the MoM-BH*-1 observation.
  • Labeled speculation: conformal cyclic cosmology; cosmological natural selection (wounded by heavy neutron stars); the black-hole-star reading of the little red dots as strong inference.
  • Original here: cascaded reality; the inverted singularity; the Feedback Equation as the named loop; conditional irreducibility.
