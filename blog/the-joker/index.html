The Joker

Cross-posted from my phone, 3/7/17, 3:30 am.

Man... I understand the philosophy behind the Joker now... ok, in those DC comics he says "why so serious"... like, in the same sense they put a joker in a deck of cards... there is no reason, and that's the point. Might as well put a middle finger and ask you why to choose a red or black card; it's all the same in the end... it's like God's way of mocking humanity for having a sense of good and evil... there is none... it's just a way for our ego to justify itself for stability in life as we think we know it. Kings kept jokers around for this reason: to know that no matter how perverse the actions they commit are, they remind them to sit back, take a breath, and laugh at this self-imposed torture we have put ourselves through, because our mind is so used to these "laws" that are purely fiction, and we keep forgetting that we are one with each other. A joker mocks the very fabric of life as we think we know it. Wrong and right we can learn from knowledge, but what is knowledge? We say knowledge is power, but is that truly the answer? Power? Is that what we need to fulfill our lives? Let's say it is for a second... the urge will then become uncontrollable, and power in our minds has no limit, and we keep feeding off this like a parasite until we find ourselves back at square one. Power takes many forms. Money, a well-known man-made virtual figment, controls power. An example of how we use our craving for power through money: Americans are raised to believe that bacon and eggs are the cornerstone to start a healthy day, but it was actually a psychologist who stemmed this idea back in the 1920s... at first it was just a social experiment: if this man could get one doctor to concur with him, it would ripple, and this mere fictitious idea would now become scientific fact, backed by doctors... and it fell just into place... to this day. But then, before this man's life came to an end, he admits on television he was paid by a bacon manufacturer to spread this news so they could get more money... and they did. But who benefited from this manipulative, egocentric behavior? The man who died with regret knowing he manipulated a whole country for something as simple as money? Or that company that profited from his manipulation? The answer is no one. A zero-sum game. A nice fucking middle finger to these meaningless actions we think will make our life complete... basically like this message I have typed. The thoughts were in my head, but me wanting to share it gave me some type of ego trip while trying to explain that it doesn't matter if I tell you or not, because if I am aware, so are we. What are thoughts but energy? We don't create energy, we share, and we don't need a damn message to do so.

Damn, I'm fucking hungry. Gunna fix me up some eggs and bacon.
