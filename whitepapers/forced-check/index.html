Forced-Check Search

A decision-search engine, tested against chess

A search engine for adversarial decisions, developed against chess because chess grades its own homework: a claimed forcing line either mates or it does not. This paper describes the search shape, rollout pruning, greedy-with-exploration queue management, and elbow-method tuning, at the level of mechanism. The repository is private by design; the engine is described here, not linked.

The search shape

The engine hunts forcing sequences: lines where every reply is compelled. Candidate branches are triaged by cheap stochastic rollouts before any expensive exact search is spent, branches whose rollouts collapse are pruned wholesale. The frontier is kept in a priority queue managed greedily with a measured dose of exploration, so the engine mostly exploits its best-looking line while retaining a budgeted chance of visiting the unfashionable one that turns out to force.

Tuning is by elbow: search parameters are swept, the yield curve is plotted, and the knee of the curve, where additional depth stops paying, sets the operating point. The sweep values and the resulting constants are the omitted absolutes.

Why chess, and why private

Chess is the testbed because verification is free and adversarial structure is real; the engine's claims are machine-checkable against the game's rules. The repository stays private while the work is competitive; the site's own link-integrity rule applies, it is described on this page and deliberately carries no link.

Deliberate omissions, this paper describes structure and mechanism, not implementation absolutes. Exact constants, derivation orders, offsets, thresholds, schedules and parameter choices are omitted by design: enough to see the shape and the work, never enough to reconstruct the keys. The repositories and live artifacts remain the reference implementations.
