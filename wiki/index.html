Mine · 8

Defined here, because nobody else defines them.

  • Cascaded reality

    Definiteness is not painted onto the world at the bottom; it accumulates, one broadcast record at a time.

  • Conditional irreducibility

    Wherever an outcome runs through a channel that writes records the way decoherence does, no computation cheaper than running it exists.

  • Feedback Equation

    The whole loop stated as one artifact: a timeless constraint, a cascade that writes records, a horizon where the ledger closes, and the closed ledger handing forward the next beginning.

  • GASPER

    A personal value-of-a-statistical-life profiler: answer willingness-to-pay and willingness-to-accept questions and watch the implied price of a life move.

  • Prime finding

    My most practical name for the whole project: the primes behave less like a skill you learn and more like an energy level you reach or don't.

  • Prime Woods cipher

    The cipher under the MIDI Project: a poem and a piece of music folded against each other and read out through the primes.

  • The MIDI Project

    One project since 2023 under several names: music, color, and number on one 64-cell board, read out through a cipher.

  • The singularity, inverted

    Read from the inside, a singularity is not where physics breaks but where the constraint saturates: one position after one event.

Standard · 62

A plain sentence each, then Wikipedia.

  • A* search

    Graph search that heads for a goal using a running guess of the distance left.

  • Acrostic

    A poem whose line initials spell something.

  • AES-GCM

    AES encryption in a mode that also proves the ciphertext wasn't touched.

  • Bekenstein bound

    The most information a region can hold for its size and energy; a black hole sits exactly at the limit.

  • Bekenstein–Hawking entropy

    A black hole carries entropy proportional to the area of its horizon: a quarter of that area in Planck units.

  • Black hole

    A region where gravity is strong enough that nothing, light included, gets back out.

  • Breadth-first search

    Explore a graph level by level, nearest first.

  • Configuration space

    The space where one point gives the positions of every particle at once: 3N dimensions for N particles.

  • Conformal cyclic cosmology

    Penrose's proposal that the infinitely expanded end of one universe is, rescaled, the big bang of the next.

  • Cosmological natural selection

    Smolin's proposal that black holes seed new universes with slightly changed constants, so universes evolve toward making black holes.

  • Decoherence

    How contact with an environment turns quantum superpositions into ordinary, classical-looking outcomes.

  • Einselection

    Environment-induced superselection: the environment keeps measuring a system, and only the states that survive that monitoring stick around.

  • Energy level

    The particular energies a bound quantum system is allowed to have; it jumps between them by absorbing or giving off exact amounts.

  • ER=EPR

    Maldacena and Susskind's conjecture that entanglement and wormholes are the same thing in two descriptions.

  • Erdős–Woods number

    A length k for which some run of k + 1 consecutive integers has every member sharing a factor with one of the two ends. The first is 16.

  • Event horizon

    The boundary of a black hole: the point of no return.

  • Explicit formula

    Riemann's formula writing the prime count as a smooth main term corrected by a sum over the zeta zeros, like a sound written as a sum of frequencies.

  • Flower of Life

    A pattern of evenly overlapping circles in sixfold symmetry, found in art across many centuries.

  • Hawking radiation

    The faint thermal glow Hawking showed every black hole must give off, so black holes slowly evaporate.

  • Hilbert–Pólya conjecture

    The idea that the zeta zeros are the energy levels of some quantum system: find its Hamiltonian and the Riemann hypothesis follows.

  • Holographic principle

    Everything inside a volume may be fully encoded on its boundary.

  • Hubble tension

    Local and early-universe measurements of the expansion rate disagree by about five sigma.

  • Lean

    A programming language and proof assistant: a theorem written in it either compiles, which means it's proved, or it doesn't.

  • Linear-feedback shift register

    A shift register whose next bit is an XOR of some of its own bits; from a short seed it walks a long, deterministic, noise-like sequence.

  • Many-worlds interpretation

    Everett's reading of quantum mechanics: every outcome happens, each in its own branch.

  • MIDI

    The 1983 standard that records music as events (which note, how hard, when) instead of as sound.

  • Möbius function

    μ(n) is 0 when n has a repeated prime factor, otherwise +1 or −1 by whether it has an even or odd number of prime factors; summed against n^s it gives 1/ζ(s).

  • Möbius inversion

    Recovers a function from its running sums over divisors, with the Möbius function as the undo.

  • Möbius strip

    A surface with one side and one edge: go once around and you come back mirrored, go twice and you're home.

  • Montgomery's pair correlation

    Montgomery's 1973 conjecture that zeta zeros repel each other exactly the way eigenvalues of large random matrices do; Dyson recognized the formula over tea.

  • Navier–Stokes

    The equations of fluid flow, and the Millennium Prize question of whether smooth 3D flow can blow up in finite time; a machine-checked proof of blowup was announced in September 2026.

  • No-cloning theorem

    An unknown quantum state can't be copied.

  • No-communication theorem

    Entanglement can't carry a message: nothing done at one end changes the statistics at the other.

  • Nonce

    A number used once, so two encryptions or two proofs never come out looking alike.

  • One-electron universe

    Wheeler's 1940 idea that every electron is the same electron zigzagging through time, its backward stretches showing up as positrons.

  • Path integral

    Feynman's quantum mechanics: add up every possible history, each weighted by its action.

  • PBKDF2

    Stretches a password into a key by hashing it many thousands of times, so every guess gets expensive.

  • Perlin noise

    Smooth, natural-looking randomness for textures and motion, developed by Ken Perlin after working on Tron.

  • Prime number theorem

    The primes thin out like 1/ln x: the number of primes up to x is roughly x/ln x.

  • Prime-counting function

    π(x), the number of primes up to x.

  • Problem of time

    Why the deepest equations have no time in them while we plainly live in it; Page and Wootters' answer is that time is a correlation between a clock and the rest.

  • QALY

    Quality-adjusted life year: a year of life weighted by its health, from 1 for full health down to 0.

  • Quantum Darwinism

    Zurek's extension of einselection: the environment broadcasts many redundant copies of the surviving state, which is why different observers agree.

  • Quantum tunnelling

    A particle crossing a barrier it classically can't climb, with odds that fall off exponentially with the barrier's width, height, and the particle's mass.

  • Quasinormal mode

    The damped tones a disturbed black hole rings with; they depend only on its mass and spin, and gravitational-wave detectors hear them as the ringdown.

  • Random matrix theory

    Matrices with random entries; the spacing of their eigenvalues models the energy levels of complex quantum systems and, unexpectedly, the zeta zeros.

  • Reed–Solomon

    Error-correcting codes that treat data as points on a polynomial, so missing or damaged pieces can be rebuilt; they're on CDs, QR codes, and deep-space links.

  • Riemann hypothesis

    The conjecture, open since 1859, that every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function lies on the line with real part 1/2.

  • Riemann zeta function

    ζ(s) = 1 + 1/2^s + 1/3^s + ..., which Euler showed is a product over the primes; where it equals zero controls how the primes are spaced.

  • SHA-256

    A cryptographic hash: any input becomes a 256-bit digest that can't feasibly be run backwards.

  • Single-event upset

    A bit flipped by a passing particle, usually from a cosmic-ray shower; harmless or catastrophic depending on which bit.

  • Singularity

    Where general relativity's equations stop returning answers: curvature diverges and paths end.

  • Singularity theorems

    Penrose and Hawking's proofs that singularities are a generic outcome of gravitational collapse, not a fluke of perfect symmetry.

  • Superposition

    A quantum system holding several possible states at once until something records which one.

  • Technological singularity

    Vinge's 1993 prediction horizon: past some point of machine intelligence, our models of the future stop returning answers.

  • Value of a statistical life

    What people will pay for a small cut in mortality risk, divided by the size of the cut: willingness to pay over change in risk.

  • Vorticity

    How much a fluid is spinning at each point.

  • Wave function

    The quantum description of a system; for N particles it lives in a 3N-dimensional configuration space, not in ordinary space.

  • Wheeler–DeWitt equation

    ĤΨ = 0: quantum mechanics applied to the whole universe, with no time in it.

  • Wormhole

    A hypothetical tunnel through spacetime joining two distant regions.

  • XOR

    Exclusive or: true when exactly one input is true. XOR a message with a key, XOR again with the same key, and the message comes back.

  • Zero-sum game

    A situation where one side's gain is exactly the other side's loss.
